# Review M1, round 1: port of miniQU to the new architecture

Reviewer verdict: **not approved**. One blocking item (B1, engine speed under
the benchmark protocol). Everything else is accepted: structure, readability,
owner constraints, soundness, proofs.

Reviewed: branch `rewrite/cadical-style` at `4191cb6` (solver sources as of
`00eb7a4`, binary sha256 `a48a1a946f47`). I read the core of `src/` in full
(`internal`, `arena`, `constraint`, `watch`, `propagate*`, `assign`,
`backtrack`, `prefix`, `analyze`, `analyze_qu`, `learn`, `model`, `collect`,
`reduce`, `decide`, `bump`, `restart`, `depend`, `heap`, `proof`,
`qlrup_tracer`, `checker`, `solver`, `encode`, `app`, `profile`, `limit`),
compared `analyze_q`, `learn` and `reduce` with the old `analyzeQ`,
`finishLearnt` and `reduceDB`, and sampled the unit tests. Not read line by
line: `analyze_ldq.cpp`, `minimize.cpp`, `check.cpp`, the parsers,
`circuit.cpp`, the test tooling.

## 1. Architecture and owner constraints: accepted

- The tree follows ARCHITECTURE 3: one technique per translation unit, a
  header comment in each that states the QBF side condition and the proof
  steps, state owned through `constraint.cpp` / `assign.cpp` /
  `backtrack.cpp`, `Solver` without solver logic, the frontend linked only
  against `miniqu.hpp` (and tested against a fake solver).
- **Arena** retained: `Arena` is MiniSat's region allocator (32-bit `Ref`,
  13/8 growth, wasted counter, copying collector). Only `constraint.hpp`
  does word arithmetic.
- **Watch lists and access-ordered layout** retained: `Watch{Ref, blocker}`
  of 8 bytes, separate clause and cube lists, lazy cleaning by smudging, and
  `garbage_collect` copies along the watch lists in variable order
  (`relocAll`); `test/unit/collect.cpp` checks that order.
- The watch loop exists once (`propagate.hpp`, template over kind and the
  lone-secondary rule); mode 2 has its own loop as the design says.
- The old `minisat/` tree is out of the root build; the frozen baseline
  binary is the reference.
- BVE side conditions: not applicable to M1.

## 2. Soundness and proofs: accepted

- `add_original`: sort, deduplicate, skip tautologies, log the original as
  given, derive the reduction from it, never simplify under root assignments
  of the other kind (INV-DERIVED). Correct.
- `qreduce` / `reduced_size` remove exactly the secondary literals right of
  every primary literal; the internal order is the prefix order.
- `analyze_q` is a faithful port of `analyzeQ`. I checked the one place that
  looked fragile (`remaining` is counted against `blocked`, which `add` may
  still lower): after the asserting literal is found every remaining pivot
  lies on a level below the pivot level, so nothing added later can be
  blocked. No defect.
- Deletions come only from `mark_garbage`; units are never deleted; the
  empty constraint is logged with its chain; a model cube under a QLRUP
  tracer is a fatal error, not a silent gap.
- Proof logging is not silently disabled anywhere. Two documented limits:
  mode 2 has no proofs (rejected at start-up), and minimisation is switched
  off while a tracer is connected (a warning is printed; M2.3 removes it).
- Own spot check with the Lean checker on benchmark instances (the
  benchmark stage produced no proofs): 6 of 6 `s VERIFIED` with the correct
  verdict: `DW-sat-08-22-1` (default), `lift_gr1+2.tlsf` (default),
  `hadw10-11` (`-dl`, 112 MB proof), `collector_v17.tlsf` (`-mode=1`),
  `chess_solving_mate_in_2_1982_WCSC-6_02` (`-mode=1 -dl`), `jctc2-pass`
  (`--reduceint=50 --tier1glue=0 --gcfrac=0`).
- The independent verification (matrix 1:300 release, 1:100 asan, QDIMACS
  1:300) found no wrong verdict, no rejected proof and no sanitizer report.
  The asan-only stress-row timeouts fall under the owner decision of
  2026-11-22 and are not held against the milestone.

## 3. Code quality: accepted

Clean, readable, CaDiCaL-like. No giant functions in what I read; the kind
value removes the clause/cube duplication of the old code; deviations from
the old solver are stated where they occur (dependency reset skipped while
nothing was learnt, no duplicate stale watch in mode 2, tie-break of
`reduce` by size instead of activity). Minor points are in section 6.

## 4. Performance evidence: B1 (blocking)

The report's verdict is "at parity within noise, losses are search-order
variance". The raw CSVs do not support the second half, and my reruns
contradict it.

**What the CSVs show.** Since T11 many instances have the *same conflict
count* in both binaries for the same seed, so they run the same search and
their CPU-time ratio measures engine speed alone. Over the instances solved
by both with identical conflict counts and more than 1 s of baseline CPU
time, new / baseline CPU time (geometric mean):

| config | seed 1 | seed 2 | seed 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| default | 1.133 (n=39) | 1.125 (n=39) | 1.058 (n=37) |
| `-dl` | 1.104 (n=40) | 1.074 (n=42) | 1.111 (n=30) |
| mode 1 | 1.140 (n=35) | | |
| mode 2 | 1.026 (n=33) | | |

In default, 40 of 48 such instances (mean over seeds) are slower; the only
faster ones are parse-dominated (`1.3`, `1.4`, `9.1`, `10.x`, `3.2`: a few
hundred conflicts) plus `jctc9-pass`. Examples with identical conflicts and
near-identical propagation counts, three seeds each: `DW-unsat-09-22-1`
15.4 s -> 25.2 s, `DW-unsat-09-23-1` 26.0 -> 34.7, `hadw10-11` 2.6 -> 3.3,
`5.1-1` 40.7 -> 50.7. This is the same sign in every configuration, and it
is what the report's own numbers hint at (two fewer solved on average in
default and `-dl`, `chess` 0 wins / 9 losses, `-dl` sign test 27 / 44, mode 1
14 / 31 with p = 0.016, which by ROADMAP 0.3 is "worse" and fails the mode-1
gate as the report itself states).

**Reproduction** (cores 0-47, seed 1, baseline `-rnd-init -rnd-seed=1`, new
`--seed=1`, user CPU time, conflict counts identical in every pair):

1. Twelve instances, 4 copies each, 48 processes of **one binary at a
   time** (the situation of a benchmark run), two rounds, median of 8:

   | instance | baseline s | new s | ratio |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | DW-sat-08-22-1 | 1.93 | 4.14 | 2.15 |
   | DW-unsat-09-22-1 | 10.92 | 15.17 | 1.39 |
   | jctc10-fail | 7.38 | 10.16 | 1.38 |
   | DW-sat-09-26-1 | 3.40 | 4.43 | 1.30 |
   | chess_solving_mate_in_3_2003_UKR-CH_04 | 9.72 | 11.64 | 1.20 |
   | 9.2 | 14.50 | 16.75 | 1.15 |
   | chess_solving_mate_in_3_2012_GRE-CH-11_02 | 12.18 | 13.91 | 1.14 |
   | chess_solving_mate_in_3_1991_WCSC-15_05 | 8.14 | 9.25 | 1.14 |
   | 4.1 | 8.01 | 8.97 | 1.12 |
   | 5.2-1 | 32.41 | 36.33 | 1.12 |
   | hadw11-12 | 6.33 | 6.51 | 1.03 |
   | ltl2dba_beta6.tlsf | 12.09 | 12.13 | 1.00 |

   Geometric mean 1.23. The ranges of the two arms do not overlap for 7 of
   the 12 instances.
2. The same kind of load with both binaries **interleaved** (24 + 24
   processes): typical ratios 1.03-1.06, `DW` 1.1-1.8, `hadw11-12` 0.99,
   `ltl2dba_beta6` 0.85.
3. Lightly loaded machine (16 processes): ratios between 0.83 and 1.15,
   about 1.03 on average.

So the new binary is a few percent slower per propagation when the machine
is idle and 10-25% slower when the other cores run copies of it. That it
suffers more among its own copies than among baseline processes points at
memory traffic (cache footprint or memory bandwidth), not at instruction
count. It also explains why the earlier measurements (header measurement in
T07/T11, 8 processes) reported propagation "not below the baseline": they
were taken on an idle machine, and T12's profile-overhead check used the
parse-dominated smoke set.

I did not find the cause (no `perf` on the machine). Candidates, in the
order I would test them: the 12-byte header on every constraint against 4
bytes for originals in the old solver (a binary original is 20 bytes instead
of 12; `DW-sat-08-22-1` has 270 000 originals and never reduces or collects
in this run, so its layout is pure parse order); `std::vector<Watch>` per
literal against MiniSat's `vec`; `VarData` as one 12-byte record; the
`stats.ticks++` store per dereferenced constraint in the watch loop; the
initial arena layout if the old solver's start-up path differs.

**Why this blocks.** M1's goal is parity, and the ROADMAP says a faithful
port has speed ratios at or above 1. A 10-25% engine slowdown under the very
protocol all later milestones are measured with is significant, it is
unexplained, and the report attributes it to variance. PAR2 hides it only
because few instances sit near the 60 s limit. This is not a request for
more seeds or longer timeouts: the existing CSVs and a five-minute rerun
show it.

**B1, to do:**

1. Find the cause. Reproduce with the arm-separated load above
   (`DW-sat-08-22-1`, `DW-unsat-09-22-1`, `jctc10-fail` are the clearest)
   and fix it, or show by measurement which retained design decision costs
   what and obtain a coordinator/owner decision in STATUS.md that accepts it.
2. Add the identical-search comparison to the paired report (instances with
   equal conflict counts in both arms: CPU-time ratio per instance,
   geometric mean, per configuration). It needs no extra runs.
3. Gate for round 2, inside the normal budget: under benchmark load the
   identical-search CPU ratio of default and `-dl` has a geometric mean of at
   most about 1.03 and no family is systematically above 1.10, or the excess
   is explained and accepted as in step 1. Mode 1 must no longer be "worse"
   by the sign test.
4. Re-measure the header decision of T07 under the same load (it was taken
   on an idle machine) if step 1 points at the constraint layout.

## 5. What the reduced evidence can and cannot show

- Verdicts: 18 runs of 376 instances without a discrepancy, plus the fuzz
  matrix. Strong.
- Proofs: fuzz matrix with Lean checking at 1:300 and my six benchmark
  proofs. The proof-coverage and overhead run on the benchmark set was
  dropped for the budget, so coverage against the baseline's table at this
  commit is not measured (the T11 smoke had 33 of 33 accepted). Not blocking
  under the budget decision; run it in round 2 if time allows.
- Speed: three seeds at 60 s cannot resolve a difference of two solved
  instances, as the report says. They do resolve the engine-speed question
  once identical searches are compared, see section 4.
- Memory: lower than the baseline on large instances (about 0.8), within the
  16 MB rule elsewhere.

## 6. Suggestions (not blocking)

- `struct Last` and `Internal::last` are read nowhere; add them with the
  first milestone that reads them.
- `reduce.cpp` and `bump.cpp` keep MiniSat's constants as named `constexpr`
  values, while ARCHITECTURE 9.1 says every heuristic constant is an option.
  Either make them options when M2 touches the schedule or relax the
  sentence.
- `Solver::freeze` only checks its argument; say so in `miniqu.hpp` until
  M3 gives it a meaning.
- Benchmark reports should state the order in which the arms ran; timings
  on this machine depend visibly on what the neighbouring cores do.
- Run modes 1 and 2 with all three seeds when the budget allows; a
  single-seed sign test is the weakest number in the report.
