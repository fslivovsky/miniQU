## 1. Architecture and owner constraints: accepted

- **Arena**: unchanged (MiniSat layout, accepted in round 2).
- **Watch lists**: `WatchList` is MiniSat's `vec<Watcher>` in size (pointer,
  32-bit size and capacity, 16 bytes), growth (`cap + cap/2 + 2`) and
  allocation (`realloc`); `Watch{Ref, blocker}` is 8 bytes; one list per
  literal and kind; lazy cleaning; collection in watch order. This is closer
  to the owner's constraint than the `std::vector` of rounds 1 and 2.
- The change stays inside `watch.hpp`. The five call sites changed
  mechanically (`push_watch (list, w)` to `list.push (w)`), nothing else in
  the core moved, and the search is the same as round 2 (1513 of 1513
  compared runs identical, ticks and proofs byte-identical on 10 of 10).
- ARCH 6.1 describes the code as built, with the history of the three
  layouts and the rule that follows from it (compare the size of what is
  touched per propagated literal with the old solver, measure with the
  benchmark placement).
- BVE side conditions: not applicable to M1.

## 2. Soundness and proofs: accepted

No reasoning code changed this round. What could break is memory handling of
the hand-written list, and that is covered: `grow` checks the 32-bit capacity
and the `realloc` result, moves leave the source empty, copy is deleted, the
destructor frees; the unit test runs growth, both `erase` forms, `clear`,
move construction, move assignment and self-assignment, and `resize` of the
record array, also in the asan tree. A push never targets the list being
compacted (the new watch is a literal other than the one just assigned), so
`i`, `j` stay valid across `realloc`.

### 4.2 The `jctc` line (1.330, n=4, default): noise, accepted

The report states the line as not met and gives a hypothesis from isolated
runs. Isolated runs do not answer a question about the loaded protocol, so I
ran the protocol again. CPU seconds, seed 1, same search in every cell:

| instance | base committed | base rep1 | base rep2 | new committed | new rep1 | new rep2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| jctc10-fail | 5.05 | 10.97 | 4.95 | 11.79 | 10.10 | 5.13 |
| jctc2-pass | 2.05 | 2.06 | 2.92 | 4.12 | 2.94 | 2.94 |
| jctc8-pass | 16.13 | 17.61 | 16.26 | 16.39 | 17.21 | 16.55 |

`jctc10-fail` is bimodal under load (about 5 s or about 11 s) **for the
baseline too**; it is the largest outlier of every same-binary pair I can
form. `gate.py` on the two baseline runs against each other gives jctc 0.840
(n=3), on the two new runs 0.788. Paired by repetition the two instances
give 0.92 and 1.04 (`jctc10-fail`), 1.43 and 1.01 (`jctc2-pass`). The
committed 1.330 paired a fast baseline run with a slow new run of
`jctc10-fail`. In isolation the new binary is 7-11% faster on both (worker's
runs). So there is no engine regression here; what remains is that
`jctc2-pass` might be a few percent to 40% slower under load (new 2.94,
2.94, 4.12 against 2.05, 2.06, 2.92), which one instance of 2-3 s cannot
decide and which does not matter for any conclusion of M1.

What this says about the gate: the per-instance standard deviation of the
log ratio between two runs of the same binary was 0.06-0.14 in my four runs
(0.075 in round 2), with tails of a factor 2 on single instances. A family
of three or four instances therefore crosses 1.10 by chance from time to
time; with a dozen gated families per round a false FAIL is to be expected
every few rounds. The gate did its job (it stopped and asked), but it needs
a rule for this case (section 6, item 1).

## 6. Follow-ups (not blocking)

1. **Record the jctc decision and give `gate.py` a noise rule.** STATUS.md
   needs a coordinator entry that closes the jctc line with reference to
   section 4.2. For later rounds: a family FAIL is confirmed or dismissed by
   one more back-to-back pair of the configuration (12 minutes), or the gate
   also prints the same-binary family ratio where a second run of the
   reference exists. A FAIL that does not repeat and whose same-binary ratio
   is as far from 1 is reported as noise; one that repeats blocks.
2. **Remove the old tree from the build.** The milestone ends with "the old
   `minisat/` tree is removed from the build once parity is shown". CMake
   does not build it, but the top-level MiniSat `Makefile` still does, and
   `minisat/` is in the tree. Do it as the first commit after this approval
   (the frozen baseline binary stays the reference), or record why it stays.
3. **Proof coverage on the benchmark set** (D7) has not been measured for
   the rewrite. Run it once at the start of M2, before new techniques change
   the proofs, so that later rounds have a number to compare with.
4. `WatchList::erase (first, last)` ignores `last` outside the assertion; a
   `truncate (first)` would say what it does. `fatal` prints the capacity
   with `%lu` and a cast; `%zu` or `PRIu64` is the usual form.
5. `bench/l3lib.sh` has `NEW_DEFAULT` pointing at `build/release/miniQU`,
   which is whatever was built there last; the scripts should print the
   sha256 of each arm into their output.
